The Forbidden Scales — The Books

Twenty main volumes. Fifteen special stories and editions. Each book building on the last, each one a step deeper into a world that keeps revealing new rooms. No spoilers. Only promises — and hooks designed to keep you up past midnight.

Book One
The Heartstonian Friendship
Five dragons from different tribes, different ranks, different worlds — thrown together by circumstances none of them chose. Before the powers, before the politics, before everything else: the moment that will either save or doom all of them. The first question the book asks is simple. Are they really friends? The answer will take twenty books to fully deliver.
The world needs them to trust each other. The world has not told them this yet.
Book Two
A Revival
Something that was believed to be over is not over. Something believed to be dead is not dead. The group, barely held together after Book One, must now contend with a threat that should not exist — and the discovery that some of what they were told about the world was deliberately, carefully wrong.
They buried it. They did not bury it deep enough.
Book Three
The True Awakening
Powers begin to shift. Things dormant come online. Things assumed understood turn out to be only partly understood. For some, this is a gift. For others, it is the thing they were most afraid of. The world gets bigger and more dangerous in equal measure.
The power was always there. The question is what it will do now that it is awake.
Book Four
Heated Rebellion
Someone has had enough. A rebellion is building — not only among the dragons, but within the group itself. Loyalties that seemed certain are tested. Old alliances crack. Fire, in both the literal and metaphorical sense, is everywhere. This is the book where the series stops being about finding your place and starts being about fighting for it.
You can only push someone so far. They pushed too far.
Book Five
The Fractured Crown
Royalty has never been safe, and now it is not even stable. The thrones of the dragon world are cracking — politically, physically, personally. The question of who deserves power — and who has simply inherited it — becomes unavoidable and unanswerable.
The crown was never a gift. It was always a trap.
Book Six
Shards of the Forgotten
